# Three MLX packages unlock Lift's media recipes

About two dozen open-weight image, video and audio models have a documented way to run on Apple Silicon, and about half of them can edit existing media as well as create it. The fastest and most reliable Mac paths mostly run on Apple's MLX framework, through **mflux** (images), **ltx-2-mlx / mlx-video** (video) and **mlx-audio** (speech and music), rather than on PyTorch. The strongest first recipes are **FLUX.2 [klein] 4B** and **Z-Image Turbo** for images (Apache-2.0, fit 16 GB Macs; klein also does instruction editing), **LTX-2.3** for video (text, image and audio to video, plus retake, extend and keyframe editing), **ACE-Step 1.5** and **Stable Audio 3 Small** for music and sound (both edit time ranges of existing audio), and **Chatterbox** and **Kokoro** for speech, with **SeedVR2** upscaling and a background remover in between to feed TRELLIS.2, Pixal3D and UniRig. Lift has to make one decision before anything else: whether to allow MLX-based recipes. Staying PyTorch-only keeps about a third of the shortlist. It loses the only working Mac video path (LTX on MPS fails on fp8 weights and produces NaNs) and most of the image editors. Reading Lift's code shows that support costs less than it looks. `lift_worker` never imports torch, install steps are free-form shell, and the worker already returns a list of output files. The schema mainly needs a way to say "this task edits its input": a mask input, a time-range setting, optional and capped inputs, an `audio_set` output, and machine-readable license restrictions. Nothing in this report was run on a Mac. Every recipe below needs the smoke test listed in the roadmap before Lift commits to it.

## PyTorch MPS breaks on video, so MLX carries most recipes

PyTorch on MPS still handles most image diffusion and audio models, but three failures keep coming back on large models. First, **MPS has no kernels for fp8**. The official LTX-2 fp8 checkpoints fail with `Undefined type Float8_e4m3fn` ([lilting.ch](https://lilting.ch/en/articles/ltx2-wan22-mac-local-video-gen); [comfyui-ltx2-mlx](https://comfy.icu/extension/tanis2000__comfyui-ltx2-mlx)). Second, MPS cannot hold a tensor of more than **2^32 elements** ([diffusers MPS docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/diffusers/optimization/mps)), which shows up as black frames and INT_MAX errors on long clips. Third, LTX-2's two-stage pipeline produced **NaNs at VAE decode** on MPS. On M1–M3 chips there is a fourth problem: bf16 runs in software emulation (hardware bf16 starts with M4). In one Qwen-Image-Edit case, a run went from about 80 seconds to about 10 minutes after a bf16 switch, and forcing fp16 produced black images ([lilting.ch](https://lilting.ch/en/articles/comfyui-qwen-mps-bf16-slowdown)). The same author's ComfyUI test of Wan 2.2 14B on an M1 Max took **82 minutes for a 2-second clip** and concluded that proper LTX-2 video on a Mac via PyTorch was "currently not feasible" ([lilting.ch](https://lilting.ch/en/articles/ltx2-wan22-mac-local-video-gen)).

MLX ports avoid these failures and are faster. On an M3 Max 64 GB, an LTX-2.5 clip took **53 seconds on MLX versus 853 seconds on PyTorch MPS** ([gist](https://gist.github.com/DanielHauschildt/9ad1d7cef482ed508a121881280b74dd/)). mflux's Qwen-Image-2.1 runs about 78 seconds for 40 steps on an M5 Max, against a diffusers-on-MPS reference of about 85 seconds ([mflux qwen21](https://github.com/mflux-community/mflux/blob/main/src/mflux/models/qwen21/README.md)). Apple reports FLUX-dev-4bit under MLX is more than **3.8× faster on M5 than M4**, because MLX uses the M5's neural accelerators ([Apple ML Research](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/exploring-llms-mlx-m5)). Three MIT-licensed packages cover most of the catalog. **mflux** offers one CLI per model for about 14 image families, edit commands included ([mflux](https://github.com/mflux-community/mflux)). **mlx-video** covers LTX-2 and Wan 2.1/2.2 ([mlx-video](https://github.com/Blaizzy/mlx-video)). **mlx-audio** covers about 20 TTS models, speech-to-text and MiniMax-Music3 ([mlx-audio](https://github.com/Blaizzy/mlx-audio)). **stable-diffusion.cpp** is the widest single native binary, covering FLUX, Qwen, Z-Image and edit models plus Wan, LTX and HunyuanVideo 1.5 on Metal ([sd.cpp](https://github.com/leejet/stable-diffusion.cpp)). Its flags "may change frequently", and one M3 user measured 600 s per iteration for Z-Image Turbo, against 19 s in ComfyUI ([sd.cpp #1030](https://github.com/leejet/stable-diffusion.cpp/issues/1030)). Three tools were ruled out as backends. ComfyUI inherits every MPS limit and would mean shipping workflow JSONs. Draw Things needs the app running. DiffusionKit was **archived on 2026-03-21** ([DiffusionKit](https://github.com/argmaxinc/DiffusionKit)).

The upstream PyTorch-on-MPS path is still right for several models: Qwen-Image-Edit through `qwen-image-mps`, SeedVR2 through the numz CLI, Chatterbox, F5-TTS, VoxCPM2, ACE-Step 1.5's `pt` backend, LivePortrait and Stable Audio 3 Small on CPU. For these, Lift's existing shim conventions apply. Set `PYTORCH_ENABLE_MPS_FALLBACK=1`. Leave the high-watermark at its default of 1.7, because setting it to 0.0 "may cause system failure" ([PyTorch MPS env vars](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/2.14/mps_environment_variables.html)). Pin non-fp8 weights explicitly, since SeedVR2's CLI defaults to a 3B fp8 model and LTX's fp8 checkpoints will not load.

## The MLX decision: allow it and keep the whole shortlist, or stay PyTorch-only and keep a third

Lift's principle is to package an unmodified upstream repo, pinned at a commit. An MLX recipe pins a *port*, such as mflux (a "line-by-line MLX port" of the diffusers code), instead of the model authors' repo. That is the same compromise Lift already accepted for trellis-mac. The code shows less is at stake than the schema suggests. `python/lift_worker/src/lift_worker/worker.py` says torch is "the launcher's choice" and never imports it. The `install:` list is arbitrary shell, so `uv pip install mflux==…` is as valid as `uv pip install torch==…`. `launcher.run()` already returns a list of output paths. An MLX package is still a Python package in a uv venv, driven from `launcher.py`. Three things actually assume PyTorch today. The worker's memory reading only queries `torch.mps`, so MLX recipes would show no memory use. The shim makes no sense without torch. And nothing stops a non-M-series Mac from installing an MLX recipe.

| Option | What it keeps | What it loses | Cost and risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. PyTorch only (status quo)** | Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 (`qwen-image-mps`), SeedVR2 (numz), Z-Image and FLUX.2 klein via Lift-written diffusers launchers, Chatterbox, Kokoro (pip `kokoro`), F5-TTS, VoxCPM2, Dia2 (CPU), ACE-Step 1.5 (`pt`), Stable Audio 3 Small (CPU), LivePortrait, the slow Wan 2.2-5B fork | **All practical video** (LTX on MPS fails; the Wan fork takes 47–97 min per clip), mflux's edit commands (Kontext, Fill, klein multi-reference, Qwen-2.1 masks), MiniMax-Music3 (upstream needs CUDA), Higgs v3 and Voxtral, MLX speed and M5 gains | No new runtime. Lift would have to write and own diffusers launcher code for image models whose upstream CLIs are unusable |
| **B. Add MLX Python packages (recommended)** | Everything in A, plus the mflux family, ltx-2-mlx / mlx-video, mlx-audio | Little. sd.cpp-only models (HiDream-O1, LongCat-Edit, FLUX.2 [dev]) stay out | A port can lag upstream features; one package pin covers many recipes, so one regression breaks many; flag semantics differ from upstream (see the mask and strength notes below) |
| **C. Also add native sd.cpp** | B, plus HiDream-O1, LongCat-Image-Edit, FLUX.2 [dev], SD3.5/SDXL/Chroma in GGUF for 16 GB Macs | — | Flags change often; edit-mode flags are undocumented for 2026; speed reports conflict. Defer until a model needs it |

The recommendation is **Option B now and Option C only on demand**. Under Option A, the video track needs Lift-owned launcher code for nearly every image model anyway, because the upstream CLIs are not runnable as shipped. Z-Image's `inference.py` hard-codes the prompt, and FLUX.2's `cli.py` is interactive and hard-codes CUDA ([Z-Image](https://github.com/Tongyi-MAI/Z-Image/blob/main/inference.py); [flux2 cli.py](https://github.com/black-forest-labs/flux2/blob/main/scripts/cli.py)). Without MLX, Lift writes more of its own code, not less.

Two conventions vary from runtime to runtime and will break an editing UI if Lift does not handle them. **Mask polarity**: diffusers and FLUX Fill repaint white and keep black, while mflux's Qwen-2.1 edit treats transparency as "keep". **Strength direction**: Lift's convention would be 1 = ignore the source. A community note claims the opposite for mflux's `--image-strength`, and also says the reference image is silently ignored unless strength is passed explicitly ([community PR](https://zff.dev/CryptoJones/MacminiM2Pro_LocalModelConfig/pulls/8)). Both conventions must be confirmed against `mflux-capabilities` output at the pinned tag.

LTX-2.3 through ltx-2-mlx is the editing centerpiece. **Retake** takes a source video, a start and end, and a prompt, and regenerates only that span. Its range is in *latent frames*, not seconds, so the launcher must convert. **Extend** takes a source video, a number of extra frames and a prompt. **Keyframe** takes reference images and interpolates between them. The repeatable `--image PATH FRAME STRENGTH` flag anchors any number of frames, each with its own strength. **IC-LoRA** takes a control video (depth, pose or edges) plus a LoRA weight and restyles the clip, but only on 2.3, because 2.5 has no official task IC-LoRAs. **A2V** turns an audio file into video. **Lip-dub** is experimental ([ltx-2-mlx](https://github.com/dgrauet/ltx-2-mlx)). On macOS 26, MLX recipes need `AGX_RELAX_CDM_CTXSTORE_TIMEOUT=1` to avoid a GPU watchdog stall of 25–130× ([gist](https://gist.github.com/DanielHauschildt/9ad1d7cef482ed508a121881280b74dd/)). LivePortrait takes a source portrait (image or video) plus a driving video or `.pkl` motion file. SeedVR2's video mode adds `--batch_size` (must be 4n+1), `--temporal_overlap` and tiled VAE flags ([numz](https://github.com/numz/ComfyUI-SeedVR2_VideoUpscaler)).

The most complete video editor, **Wan2.1-VACE**, takes `--src_video --src_mask --src_ref_images --prompt` for masked inpaint/outpaint and subject swap ([VACE](https://github.com/ali-vilab/VACE)). It stays on the watch-list because nobody has shown it running on MPS or MLX. No instruction-based video editor ("make it night") with a Mac path was found. Video recipes should carry an "M3 Max or newer recommended" note: the M1-era numbers run to tens of minutes per clip.

### Audio edit modes and their inputs

**ACE-Step 1.5** has the richest set, documented as task types in its inference API ([INFERENCE.md](https://github.com/ace-step/ACE-Step-1.5/blob/main/docs/en/INFERENCE.md)). `repaint` takes `src_audio`, `repainting_start`, `repainting_end` (−1 means the end of the file) and a caption. `cover` takes `src_audio`, a caption, optional lyrics and `audio_cover_strength`, where 1.0 stays close to the source and about 0.1 is loose, which is the opposite of the diffusion strength convention. `lego` (add a track), `extract` (isolate a track) and `complete` take `src_audio` plus an instruction naming one of 12 tracks (vocals, drums, bass, guitar, keyboard, strings and so on). These three need the base model, not turbo. `reference_audio` steers style. The one catch is the interface. The CLI is a wizard that `-c file.toml` makes non-interactive ([CLI.md](https://github.com/ace-step/ACE-Step-1.5/blob/main/docs/en/CLI.md)), and nobody has documented whether edit tasks run that way, so the launcher may call `acestep.inference.generate_music` directly.

**Stable Audio 3 Small** is the only model whose edits are plain CLI flags ([SA3](https://github.com/Stability-AI/stable-audio-3)). Audio-to-audio uses `--init-audio` with `--init-noise-level` (e.g. 0.9; higher means more change) and a style prompt. Inpainting uses `--inpaint-audio`, `--inpaint-start` and `--inpaint-end` in seconds, and passing lists regenerates several separate regions in one pass. Continuation sets the inpaint start to the source length and asks for a longer duration. Medium (1.4B) is excluded: it needs flash-attn 2, and "silent static output on Medium usually means flash-attn is broken".

**F5-TTS word replacement** needs exactly what an editing UI would collect. It takes the source audio, the *full edited transcript*, a list of `[start, end]` second ranges to rewrite (e.g. `[1.42, 2.44], [4.04, 4.9]`) and an optional `fix_duration` per range. All of these are **hard-coded module variables, not CLI flags** ([speech_edit.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SWivid/F5-TTS/main/src/f5_tts/infer/speech_edit.py)), so the recipe's `launcher.py` must set them. Lift should pair it with a local speech-to-text step (Whisper through mlx-audio) to produce the transcript and the word timestamps the ranges come from. **Chatterbox voice conversion** (`example_vc.py`) takes a source recording plus a target-voice clip. Nobody has checked whether the script reads arguments or hard-codes its paths, or whether the mlx-audio port includes VC. **Continuation** (VoxCPM2's reference audio plus transcript; Dia2's `--prefix-speaker-1/-2`) extends an existing clip rather than changing it. Every cloning or conversion recipe should show a consent notice. Chatterbox's watermark makes it the safest default for that job.

**No video-to-audio (foley) model has evidence of running on a Mac.** MMAudio is documented as "tested only on Ubuntu" ([MMAudio](https://github.com/hkchengrex/MMAudio)). So nothing yet adds sound to Lift's generated videos except LTX's joint audio track and a plain mux of an ACE-Step or Stable Audio track.

## Seven schema additions cover every edit mode the research found

The current schema already covers more than it seems. `image_set` handles multi-reference editing. `text` carries instructions and transcripts. Pixal3D already consumes an `image_set` with a `four_view` layout. Attribution components carry free-text `restrictions` (TRELLIS.2 uses one for "Built with DINOv3"). `memory_overrides` keyed on a setting already expresses quantization tiers. What is missing is listed below, smallest change first.

| Addition | Shape | Why (evidence) |
|---|---|---|
| **1. `runtime`** | Recipe-level enum `torch` (default) \| `mlx` \| `native`. `mlx` means no shim allowed, an Apple Silicon check, worker memory read from MLX instead of `torch.mps`, and `AGX_RELAX_CDM_CTXSTORE_TIMEOUT=1` by default. `native` adds a `binary: {url, sha256}` fetched at install | mflux, mlx-video, mlx-audio and ltx-2-mlx are Python but not torch; sd.cpp is a binary |
| **2. `post_download`** | Shell steps run after the weights land in the cache | mlx-video's Wan path needs `convert_wan` once after download, and the installer currently runs install steps *before* weights |
| **3. `edit` on a task** | `edit: { source: <input name>, mode: <mode> }`, with modes `img2img`, `instruct`, `inpaint`, `outpaint`, `upscale`, `cutout`, `control`, `retake`, `extend`, `keyframe`, `motion_transfer`, `voice_convert`, `word_replace`, `continue`, `cover`, `stem_add`, `stem_extract`. **One task per mode**, which matches how the CLIs split them (`-fill` vs `-kontext`, ACE-Step task types, ltx-2-mlx subcommands) | Lets Lift choose the dedicated editor widget and pre-fill the source from the item being edited |
| **4. `mask` input type** | `{ name: mask, type: mask, of: image, mask_format: white_edit \| alpha_keep }`. Lift always stores grayscale at source size, white = change, and converts for `alpha_keep`. Optional `feather` | Diffusers and FLUX Fill: white = repaint. mflux Qwen-2.1: transparent = keep. VACE: a mask *video* (`of: video`) |
| **5. `optional` and `max` on inputs** | `{ name: references, type: image_set, optional: true, max: 10 }` | Qwen-2.1 takes up to 10 references, Qwen-Edit 1–3 optimal, Kontext 1. Reference audio and ACE-Step style audio are optional |
| **6. `time_range` setting type** | `{ key: span, type: time_range, of: audio, multiple: true }`. Always seconds in the UI; the launcher converts (ltx-2-mlx wants latent frames). Plus `meaning: change \| keep` on strength-like floats, so every slider reads "how much to change" | ACE-Step repaint, SA3 inpaint (several regions), F5 `parts_to_edit` (several), LTX retake. Strength runs in opposite directions in ACE-Step cover vs SA3 vs diffusers |
| **7. `audio_set` output, and set outputs generally** | `output: audio_set` (and allow `output: image_set`) | Stem separation returns 4–12 files; MV-Adapter returns 6 views. The worker already returns a list (`worker.py` `outputs=[...]`) |

Beyond the seven, license facts should become machine-readable fields so the UI can warn before a non-developer downloads something they cannot sell. Each attribution component would gain `use: { commercial: yes | no | capped, revenue_cap_usd, display_name, consent, watermark }`. Real values from this research: Qwen-2.1 and F5-TTS `commercial: no`; LTX `capped` at $10M; SA3 and Krea 2 `capped` at $1M; MiniMax-Music3 `display_name: "MiniMax-Music3"` with a $20M threshold; Chatterbox `consent: voice, watermark: perth`. The existing `memory_overrides` should be used to declare each recipe's tiers (e.g. `when: {quantize: 4}, memory_gb: 8`), along with a free-text `resources.recommended_chip` for video and for bf16-heavy models on M1–M3. An edit task written with these additions:

```yaml
- id: inpaint
  modality: image
  label: Repaint part of an image
  edit: { source: image, mode: inpaint }
  inputs:
    - { name: image, type: image }
    - { name: mask, type: mask, of: image, mask_format: alpha_keep }
    - { name: references, type: image_set, optional: true, max: 10 }
    - { name: prompt, type: text }
  output: image
  settings:
    - { key: strength, label: How much to change, type: float, default: 0.8, min: 0, max: 1, meaning: change }
```

Keyframes need no new type yet. Each LTX anchor's image, frame and strength can travel as an `image_set` plus a launcher-parsed setting until a second model needs the same thing.

## Roadmap: ship the 16 GB chain feeders first, then gate the heavy editors

Recipes are ordered by four tests: the recipe runs on 16–32 GB Macs, its license is safe for non-developers, it feeds the existing 3D recipes, and it exercises a new schema feature once.

|---|---|---|---|

| **1. SeedVR2: force a GGUF/fp16 model over the fp8 default, check for an MPS memory leak across consecutive jobs in a long-lived worker, and confirm alpha survives. **BiRefNet: confirm it runs on MPS at all**, and compare its matte with TRELLIS.2's built-in RMBG-2.0 |
| **1. Speech** | Kokoro (mlx-audio), Chatterbox (create + VC) | Permissive, small, first audio output | Kokoro: espeak-ng through the `brew:` field. Chatterbox: whether `example_vc.py` takes arguments; whether the mlx-audio port exposes VC; real-time factor on 16 GB; watermark present |

The chains with the existing 3D recipes need no new types, because every hop is image to image until the mesh. **Photo or prompt → FLUX.2 klein / Z-Image / Qwen-Edit-2511** (e.g. "the object alone, plain white background, front view") **→ BiRefNet cut-out → SeedVR2 upscale of small crops (alpha kept) → TRELLIS.2 or Pixal3D → UniRig**. A second chain uses Pixal3D's multi-view task. Qwen-Image-Edit-2511's built-in new-viewpoint effect ([HF](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen-Image-Edit-2511)) could produce front, right, back and left views for Pixal3D's `image_set` `four_view` layout. Whether those generated views are consistent enough for reconstruction is untested and makes a good Phase 2 experiment. In the other direction, a generated image feeds LTX or Wan I2V, then SeedVR2 video upscale. Chatterbox narration feeds LTX's A2V. A turntable render of a TRELLIS.2 mesh could serve as an LTX-2.3 IC-LoRA control video for restyling, though Lift has no mesh-to-video renderer today.

## Conclusion

The research changes what kind of decision Lift faces. It started as a model-selection question. It ended as a runtime-policy question, and that policy is cheaper to adopt than the schema suggested: the worker is already torch-agnostic, so "MLX support" means a runtime flag, a memory reading and an Apple Silicon check, not a second architecture. The hard engineering is in editing semantics, not runtimes. Mask polarity, the direction of strength, seconds versus latent frames, and one-task-per-mode splits differ in every CLI. If Lift normalizes them once in the schema (white means change, strength means change, ranges are in seconds) and converts per recipe, its editing UI can stay the same across image, video and audio.

Two gaps will shape the product more than any single model. There is no Mac-runnable foley model and no Mac-runnable instruction-based video editor, so "edit this video by describing the change" and "add sound to this clip" stay out of reach on a Mac for now. LTX retake and ACE-Step repaint are the closest substitutes. And the commercially clean options thin out sharply as quality rises: the #1 open image model, the best word-replacement TTS and the best edit-drift model are all non-commercial. That makes the machine-readable `use` flags a requirement for non-developer users, not a nice-to-have.
